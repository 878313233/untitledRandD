        The Size of Perceived Athleticism and Illness as seen from Deviant Bodies
                - the idea that Athleticism is an attribute that is perceived by an indicator of heigh and size and not by recorded metrics 
                - the idea that slender qualities in some cultures are attributed to genetics while being slender in other cultures is an attribute of addiction or illness
                - "polarized napping", experiment

                If found napping on a desk in a college setting, a passerby may experience empathy if the individual depicts or is known to hold healthy attributes (wearing a blazer, student body rep, accredited person, etc) as the nap is postulated to be as a result of productive activities
                However, if an individual is found napping on a desk with attributes that point towards a lack of hygiene or mental health (dirty laundry, dark clothing, messy hair, ), a passerby may feel danger or a sense of surveillance and enforcement as a passerby may assert the nap being a result of a drug induced state.

                This same phenomena can be seen when individuals are seen laying on the floor in a suit in tie, vs in normal clothes in an experiment called "The Bystander Effect"

        Theories

Theory One
“Anytime a population or an individual with extremely low social and cultural capital starts to establish more connections, income, and legislation than those with the same or higher social and cultural capital, those individuals and groups become discredited and characters are assassinated. It’s more commonly seen when a person or a group considers themselves better or worse than someone else’s across any geographical setting or historical period.”

Theory Two
“When an imperialist attempts to leverage a government or an organization, whether it’s through infrastructure or diplomacy, it makes them feel as if they have the right to leverage the regulators and citizens too— forcing the original government and it’s citizens to look and behave identically to the one that leverages it.”

Theory Three
“Certain racial and cultural demographics in America became aware of their own social and cultural capital, which prompted them to sociologically position ( and at times semiotically regulate ) their epistemologies as a means to access, develop, or regulate a specific infrastructure or demographics, while those who weren’t aware were subject to co-optation and instrumentalization— subverting critical theories that were made to be understood rather than exploited.”

Theory Four
"If a foreign country aims to capitalize on their strategic essentialism in another so it furthers regulatory capture on behalf of their race, interest convergences install bureaucrats as a means to create laws that have never been made in those countries before."
