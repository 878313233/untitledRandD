        Theories

Theory One
“Anytime a population or an individual with extremely low social and cultural capital starts to establish more connections, income, and legislation than those with the same or higher social and cultural capital, people get arrested, discredited, and assassinated. It’s more commonly seen when a person or a group considers their race better or worse than someone else’s across any geographical setting or historical period.”

Theory Two
“When an imperialist attempts to leverage a government or an organization, whether it’s through infrastructure or diplomacy, it makes them feel as if they have the right to leverage the regulators and citizens too— forcing the original government and it’s citizens to look and behave identically to the one that leverages it.”

Theory Three
“Certain racial and cultural demographics in America became aware of their own social and cultural capital, which prompted them to sociologically position ( and at times semiotically regulate ) their epistemologies as a means to develop or regulate a specific infrastructure or demographic, while those who weren’t aware were subject to co-optation and instrumentalization— subverting critical theories that were made to be understood rather than exploited.”

Theory Four
"If a foreign country aims to capitalize on their strategic essentialism in another so it furthers regulatory capture on behalf of their race, interest convergences install bureaucrats as a means to create laws that have never been made in those countries before."
